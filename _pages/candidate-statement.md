Education changes the world, and I have witnessed technology at the center of that change. As technology transforms education, my research asks how it can support learning without becoming the learning itself. I first confronted this question as an early-career teacher in Seoul when COVID-19 began, and I now pursue it in the era of AI. Across these shifts, I have learned in practice that learning should come first and technology second (Kolb, 2017). This principle has shaped my broader aim to design technology-supported learning experiences that scaffold learners’ thinking toward greater independence. I am particularly interested in young learners and novices, including pre-service teachers (PSTs), who are still developing the knowledge and strategies needed to become independent learners. My goal is to become a faculty member at a research university who supports these learners across a continuum of scaffolds, from embodied learning that makes abstract ideas tangible to self-regulated learning that helps learners manage their own thinking with AI, while opening the black box of how learners think and learn along the way.
