Juhyun Park is a third-year Ph.D. student in Instructional Systems Technology at Indiana University Bloomington and a former public elementary school teacher in Seoul, South Korea. Her research focuses on how learners think and learn with technology and how those processes can inform the design of learning environments. She investigates this question through two lines of research: **embodied and tangible learning**, where she studies how learners reason with physical representations of abstract concepts, and **GenAI-supported learning**, where she studies how learners evaluate, use, and regulate cognitive support from generative AI. Across both lines, she designs learning environments, examines learners' processes within them, and uses those observations to inform subsequent design. Her work focuses particularly on young learners and novices, including pre-service teachers.
As a graduate researcher on NSF-funded projects including SILICON (Semiconductor Informal Learning Initiative through Community-Oriented Networks) and AI Goes Rural, she co-designs and studies technology-supported learning environments with K–12 educators. Her current first-author design-based research examines **how pre-service teachers regulate their use of AI-generated feedback during lesson planning**. She teaches EDUC-W200: Teaching with Technology and has served as a teaching assistant for EDUC-R521: Instructional Design and Development. She also contributes to the IST community through graduate student leadership, having served as the 2026 IST Conference Chair and currently serving as President of the Graduate Students of Instructional Systems Technology (GIST).
